Threads becomes a full-height side panel with real tabs, opened from the top-right Inbox button with its counts; threads started on a thing keep floating beside it.

- Ticket: `kit-89-threads-sidebar` (feature `dashboard-cleanup`, stories DCL-S3 and DCL-S4, kind build, size M, risk normal)
- Waits on: `kit-88-side-peek` (and through it `kit-87-pages-and-sidebar`): kit-87 gives the frame (`shell` on `<body>`, `.main`, `.pagebar` with `.tt`, one page at a time, the router on `parseHead`/`stampFor`/`closeTarget`/`withPage`, `talk/` over its subject's page, the `<page>?th=inbox → inbox` redirects); kit-88 gives the peek frame (`peek-open`, the drawer's slide, `overlay-open` while the viewer, a preview or a review is up, and the mirror `#tt-peek` on `<body>` that stands in for `.tt` while a peek is open)
- Consumes: `docs/devteam/dashboard/shell.md@1` (tokens `--threads-w`, `--bar-h`, `--panel-pad`, `--backdrop`, `--ease`, `--dur`, `--line-2`; the Threads frame `.threads`, `.threads-head`, `.threads-body`, `.thread-list`; `threads-open` on the `.shell` element; `tabBar`, `bindTabBar`, `threadsTabs`, `.seg`; the URL keys `th` and `t`; `withPage`; the measure). Provides no contract. Neither shared block changes: everything here is the page's own CSS and JS, outside `devteam-layout` and `devteam-shell-js`.

- Written ahead of the build. The build re-reads this spec and the plan against main once kit-87 and kit-88 have merged; anything they landed differently from what is quoted here is reconciled then (and a real conflict goes to the supervisor).

## 1. Why

Today the owner reaches threads through a small dock pill at the bottom right (`#tl-pill`) that opens a 320px panel (`#tl-panel`): every open thread, numbered as its pin, then a Resolved fold-out. A thread with no subject opens in a card stuck to the dock. The owner settled the replacement in the mock (threads `t-20261115-010826979-owner`, `t-20261115-013510326-owner`, `t-20261114-230400232-owner`, `t-20261115-020154848-owner`): one full-height panel on the right with real tabs, opened from the top-right Inbox button, which also tells them at a glance whether anything waits on them; a thread with no subject read in the panel; and threads started on a thing still floating beside it. The page moves over to make room, and a side peek stops at the panel's edge, so the owner can talk about what they are reading.

## 2. What changes (index.html only, plus tests)

### 2.1 The panel

- **Markup.** One `<aside class="threads" id="threads" aria-label="Threads">`, a child of `<body>` outside `.app` (so it also shows in the `/view/` tab, where `.app` is hidden; §2.8), holding:
  - `.threads-head` (the block's: `--bar-h` tall, `--panel-pad`, bottom border `--line`): `#th-tabs`, the tab bar, then `.end` with three icon buttons: Pin (`#tl-pinbtn`, today's pin mode, `aria-pressed`), New thread (`[data-tl-new]`), and close × (`#th-close`, `aria-label="Close Threads"`), last, so it ends `--panel-pad` from the panel's right edge;
  - `.threads-body#th-body`, holding two children: `#th-pane`, the selected tab's pane, which `threadsTabs.render` and every redraw rewrite; and `#th-card-slot` (`no-shell`), where the in-panel thread card lives (§2.4), which no pane rewrite touches.
- **Open and closed.** The panel is open while the route's page head carries `th` (§2.5). Open: `threads-open` on the `.shell` element (`document.body`); the block slides `.threads` in with `--ease`/`--dur`, and gives `.main` a right margin of `--threads-w`, so the page moves left by `--threads-w`; with a peek open, the drawer's and scrim's `right` become `--threads-w` too, so the peek's right edge meets the panel's left edge and the scrim covers only the page. Below 900px `--threads-w` is `100vw` and the block drops the margin, so the panel overlays the page. Nothing here restyles the block's frame; the page only adds and removes `threads-open`.
- **The tabs**, registered once through the block's `threadsTabs.register` and drawn with `tabBar({ tabs: threadsTabs.list() + waiting flags, selected })`, bound with `bindTabBar`:
  1. `threads`, label Threads, `count()` the number of your-move threads (§2.3), drawn blue (`waiting: true`); `null` (no number) when none is your move;
  2. `supervisor`, label Supervisor, `disabled`;
  3. `shell`, label Shell, `disabled`.
  Disabled tabs can't be selected (the block skips them). `tabBar` writes no `title`, so the page sets the disabled tabs' titles on the drawn buttons after each draw ("The supervisor's terminal: the follow-on goal", "A plain shell at the repo root: the follow-on goal"); no block change. `kit-100` registers Inbox before Threads; nothing here assumes Threads is first except today's `selected` default.
- **The Threads pane** (`threadsTabs.render('threads', el)`), top to bottom:
  - `#ask-threads`: what the supervisor's terminal is asking, today's `askHtml()` card, drawn by `askDraw()` (which today writes `#ask-dock`; the list of targets becomes `ask-inbox`, `ask-threads`, `tl-c-ask`). So the terminal's question is at the top of Threads, where the dock had it; shown with the list only (hidden while a thread is open in the panel, whose card has its own copy in `#tl-c-ask`);
  - the Open / Resolved split button: the block's `.seg` (`role="group"`, `aria-label="Which threads"`), two buttons `[data-thf="open"]` and `[data-thf="resolved"]` with `aria-pressed`, each with its count: Open, every open thread; Resolved, the resolved ones listed (today's last 50 by `resolved_at`);
  - the list (`ul.thread-list#tl-list`, the block's `--line-2` rule between items, none after the last) of the picked threads (§2.3), or one empty line ("No open threads. Hold Shift and click anything on the page, or start one with +." / "Nothing resolved yet.");
  - or, in place of the split button and the list, one thread (§2.4).
- **Redraws.** Two kinds. A *change of view* (the route: opening or leaving a thread, Open / Resolved, opening the panel) draws `#th-pane` and `#th-tabs` at once, whatever is focused, then places the focus, only when the change came from a control in the panel or the Inbox button (`thGo` called by a click or key there): an opened in-panel thread's back arrow (its composer for a + draft), the thread's row after leaving it, the pressed Open / Resolved button after a switch, the selected tab after opening. A + draft becoming its thread (`t=<id>` added after the first Send) keeps the focus where it is, as `tlCard` does today. Any other route (a reload, the first threads read landing, Back or Forward, a floating card closed) moves no focus, except that a focus left inside a panel that closes goes to the visible Inbox button (§2.9). Every pane draw calls `askDraw()` so `#ask-threads` is refilled; the `.tl-panel .ask` sizing moves to `#ask-threads .ask`. A *refresh* (the threads poll, the 10-second render, a count change) rewrites `#th-pane` only when its HTML changed and nothing in it is focused or pressed (today's `tlHeld` rule for the dock list, kept), so the 10-second redraw and the threads poll never drop a focus, a press or half-typed words. `#th-tabs` follows the same rule: redrawn only when its HTML changed and no tab in it is focused or pressed, so a count that changes doesn't take the focus off a tab. The card in `#th-card-slot` is never rewritten by a pane draw (§2.4).
- **The panel is the layer's own.** Today the layer treats what is inside `#tl` as its own (`tlOwn = closest('#tl')`): a press there is held (`TL.pressed`, read by `tlHeld`), a focus leaving it redraws the list and pins, Escape with the focus there closes the card, the Shift-point click capture lets its clicks through, the pointer over it shows no pin, and a field there doesn't hold the page's 10-second redraw (`holding()`), since that redraw rebuilds nothing of the layer. All of those are right for the panel too, so `tlOwn` becomes `closest('#tl, #threads')`, `holding()`'s `#tl` exception becomes `#tl, #threads` (the page keeps redrawing, and the counts keep moving, while the owner types in the panel), and `render()`'s draft keeping (`textarea[data-q]`, `input[data-reason]`, `input[data-overrule]`, `data-rulebox`, `data-auto`), which skips fields inside `#tl`, skips fields inside `#threads` too: the in-panel card draws a question asked in its thread with the same fields as the Inbox's card, and the two copies must not overwrite each other's half-typed words or picks. One use differs: `tlHitAt` skips own elements to find what is under the pointer, which over the panel (it overlays the page below 900px) would find the page behind it; so `tlHitAt` gives no subject when the topmost element at the point is in `#threads`. The panel is never a thread target.

### 2.2 The top-right Inbox button

kit-87's `.pagebar .tt` (and kit-88's mirror `#tt-peek`, which copies `.tt`'s content) reads:

- the inbox icon and "Inbox";
- the red count of open questions (kit-87's, unchanged);
- the thread count, `span.n.th` with one class for the most pressing state (owner, `m-20261115-011711428-supervisor`):
  - `ok` (green): when any open thread is ready to accept, it counts the ready ones plus the your-move ones; title "N ready to accept, M your move" (", M your move" only when M > 0);
  - else `you` (blue): when any is your move, it counts those; title "M your move";
  - else `wait` (grey): it counts the open threads, all waiting on the supervisor; title "N waiting on the supervisor";
  - none when there is no open thread.
  Each count is in the button's accessible name (`aria-label`, for example "Inbox: 3 open questions, 2 ready to accept, 1 your move"), since colour alone carries the state. The mirror `#tt-peek` copies `.tt`'s `aria-label` and `title` as well as its content (kit-88 copies the content; this ticket adds the two attributes to what its observer copies), so the button the owner reaches always carries them.
- Every open thread counts, floating ones (a subject on the page, an artifact or a preview) included.
- **Pressing it** opens the panel on Threads (`th=open`; until `kit-100` adds the Inbox tab before it, design § Threads). A `th=inbox` head still goes to the Inbox page through kit-87's redirect entries, which stay until `kit-100`.
- **While the panel is open the button hides** (`visibility: hidden` on `.tt` and `#tt-peek` under `.shell.threads-open`, so it is neither seen nor reached by keyboard), and the panel's × closes the panel (owner, thread `t-20261115-013510326-owner`).
- The counts are drawn wherever the threads or the questions are redrawn (`tlDraw()`, the 10-second `render()`), only when they changed (a button rewritten on every poll would drop a press).

### 2.3 The list and its three states

From today's thread data only (`/api/threads`, as `TL.threads` holds it with the owner's pending presses laid over); nothing new is stored. An open thread is in exactly one state, by the rule `kit-84` uses for the hub's `threads: { you, waiting, ready }`, so the button here and the project's hub tab always agree:

| State | When | Mark | Headline |
|---|---|---|---|
| ready to accept | `t.ready` (the supervisor proposed resolving it and the owner has said nothing since) | a small green check, `.th-st.ok` (`icon-check`) | regular |
| your move | else `t.waiting === 'owner'` (the supervisor spoke last, or there are no turns) | a filled blue dot, `.th-st.you` | bold |
| waiting on the supervisor | else | a hollow grey dot, `.th-st.wait` | regular |

`tState(t)` (one function, in the page's own JS outside `threads-core`, so the vm tests of `threads-core` are untouched) gives the state; the button's count, the Threads tab's count and the marks all read it. Each mark is `role="img"` with an `aria-label` and a `title` that say the state ("Ready to accept", "Your move", "Waiting on the supervisor"); there is no explanatory line under a row (owner, `m-20261115-011334263-supervisor`).

Read marks: today's per-browser `devteam.thread.seen` drives the dock's unread dot. The owner's three states replace that dot, and "your move" follows the server's rule (the supervisor spoke last, read or not) so the counts match the hub, which can't see a browser's marks (kit-84's spec, F1-4). The marks stay written (the card still calls `tlSeen`); nothing reads them for the list. This narrows the design's wording ("a new reply they haven't read"; inputs "who spoke last, read marks, and a ready proposal"), so it was asked (§6 Q3) and ruled yes (`m-20261115-205924178-supervisor`); if the ruling is to use the marks, "your move" becomes `waiting === 'owner'` and `tUnread(t, tlSeenAt(t.id))`, and the ruling also says how a read thread that still waits on the owner shows, since none of the three marks fits it. 

**The headline** is the thread's title (`t.title`, which the kit keeps to a line), where the mock shows the latest turn: the title is what the owner named the thread, and a turn can be 2,000 bytes.

**A row** (`li > button.th-row[data-tl-open="<id>"]`, one button per row so the list is a list of controls): the mark; for a thread with a pin on the page (a subject `tPins` numbers), the pin's number in a `.pinchip`; the subject line (`tWhere(subject)`, "no subject" left out for a subjectless thread, plus " · subject gone" when an item's subject isn't drawn, as today); the time since its last activity (`activity_at`, short form); and the headline, its title (`t.title`), one line truncated. Resolved rows carry no state mark and no pin. Open rows are ordered newest activity first (today's order); the pin numbers are by when each began (today's `tPins`), so a row's number is its pin's.

### 2.4 Opening a thread

- **Panel or floating** is one rule, `tlInPanel(t)`, decided by whether the subject exists anywhere the dashboard draws, never by whether it is on the page shown now (kit-87 draws every section each redraw, shown or not, so `tlHome` finds an item on any page): a thread is in-panel when it has no subject, or its item subject has no element (`tlHome(s)` null: the `gone` case, which the row also marks " · subject gone"), or its page subject's section doesn't exist; in the `/view/` tab, also when its subject is not this stage's (a dashboard item or page, or another artifact or preview), since that tab has no page to go to. Every other thread floats: its card opens beside its pin on its subject's page. `tlTargetRect` is only where a floating card is placed.
- **An in-panel thread** opens **in the panel**: the pane shows, in place of the split button and the list, a back arrow (`button[data-tl-back]`, `aria-label="Back to the threads"`) with the thread's title, then today's card body (turns, the ready proposal, Resolve, Reopen, the composer, its errors, `#tl-c-ask`). This is today's `#tl-card`, moved: `tlCard()` reparents the one `#tl-card` element into `#th-card-slot` (class `in-panel`) or back into `#tl` (floating), so every id and handler it has keeps working and there is never a second composer. It moves only when its place changes (floating ↔ panel), never on a plain redraw, since re-appending an element blurs what it holds; while it is in the panel, `#th-pane` holds only the back arrow row (the split button and the list aren't drawn). `#th-card-slot` is `no-shell`, so the shell's block rules don't reach the card and it keeps today's look; the page's `.tl-card.in-panel` rule overrides the floating card's `position: absolute`, `width: 360px`, `z-index`, border, shadow and radius (static, full width of the slot, no frame), and the `max-height` (the slot scrolls). The arrow goes back to the list (§2.5). In the panel the card's own title (`#tl-c-title`) and × (`[data-tl-close]`) are hidden, since the pane's row above it holds the back arrow and the title, and the panel's × closes the panel; its actions (`#tl-c-acts`: Resolve, Reopen) stay shown and work, in the card's head row at the right; the card drops `role="dialog"` while it is in the panel (it is part of the panel, not a dialog over the page) and gets it back when it floats.
- **A new thread from the panel** (+): the pane shows the same in-panel card as a draft with no subject (`TL.draft`, in memory: the URL doesn't change, and a reload shows the list; the words are kept by today's draft store, keyed `new:none`), focused on its composer. Its first Send starts the thread (today's `tlSend`); while the server's id is pending the pane keeps the draft card, and once `/api/threads` has the thread, `thGo` adds `t=<id>` as a new entry, so Back goes to the list the + was pressed on (the draft itself was never an entry).
- **A thread with a subject on the page** (Shift+click, the pin mode, or a Start a thread button of kit-88's) keeps today's floating `#tl-card` beside its numbered pin, placed by `tPlace` with the window narrowed by `--threads-w` while the panel is open above 900px (the call site passes `innerWidth − 380`, read from the token; `tPlace` itself is unchanged), so the card never sits under the panel. It is also listed (§2.3) with its pin and subject. While its card is open, its row is marked (`aria-current="true"`, `.on`), and the panel, if open, stays on the list.
- **Clicking a floating thread's row** goes to its subject and opens its card there, as one new entry: the subject's page head (as kit-87's `tlGoTo` computes it, with its Loop tab) with `t=<id>` and, above 900px, the panel's `th` kept, so the panel stays open on the list beside the page; below 900px, where the panel covers the page, `th` is dropped, so the panel closes and the owner sees the pin and the card. Then the subject scrolls into view and the card opens; on an artifact or preview subject, today's stage behaviour (`#artifact/…`, `#preview/…`). Its pin is marked while the card is open.
  - **"Its page and peek"** (done-when 2). In the mock a thread can be pinned inside a peek (a paragraph of the Constitution's file peek), and the row reopens that peek. In the kit no subject lives inside a peek: the layer finds no subject in the drawer (`dashboard.test.mjs`: "Shift held over the drawer shows no pin"), and kit-88's Start a thread pins to the record's item, whose home is a page. So every kit subject is on a page, and the row goes to that page; a peek open when the row is clicked closes by that navigation. The peek half applies once a subject can live in a peek (a later ticket that adds one reopens it here). Ruled yes (§6 Q4, `m-20261115-205924178-supervisor`); AC2 proves it.
- **A pin** still opens its thread's card in place (today's `[data-tl-pin]`).
- **Under 640px** the floating card is today's bottom sheet; the in-panel card fills the panel.

### 2.5 The URL, history and the remembered state

The `th` and `t` keys of a page head (shell.md §6), on every page:
- `th=open` or `th=resolved`: the panel is open on Threads with that list. (`th=inbox` is kit-87's redirect to the Inbox page until `kit-100`.)
- `t=<id>`: the open thread. A thread shown in the panel has `th` too; a floating thread's card may be open with the panel closed (`t` alone).

**Each change is a history entry** (shell.md §7): opening or closing the panel, picking Open or Resolved, opening a thread (from the list, a pin, or the in-panel back arrow back to the list) each assign `location.hash` to the current page head with the new `th` and `t`, so Back walks them. The router (kit-87's `route()`) reads them on every page head and draws the panel and the card from them: the router, not the click, opens and closes things, so Back, Forward and a reload all go through one path.
- Back after opening a thread returns to the list (`th` kept, `t` gone). The back arrow is a new entry too (the list's head), not `history.back()`: the thread may have been reached by a link or a reload, with no list below it; so Back after the arrow reopens the thread, as Back does after any other move.
- Closing a floating card (×, Escape) removes `t` with a new entry; a card opened by a `talk/` link keeps kit-87's close (`closeTarget`).
- **Under a link head** (a peek, the viewer, a preview, a review, a `talk/` card: any entry whose head isn't a page head, so has no state keys), a Threads change that stays on the page (opening or closing the panel, Open / Resolved, an in-panel thread, the back arrow) updates `history.state.page` with the block's `withPage` (by `replaceState`) and adds no entry, and the router redraws the panel from it, leaving the link head (and so the peek or viewer) open; a reload reopens the peek over its page with the panel open on that thread, since kit-87's router shows the recorded page head (with its `th`, `t`) under it. Closing the peek returns to that page head, panel and thread still open. A floating row's click is not such a change: it navigates to its subject's page (§2.4), a new entry, which closes the peek. The panel's state is always read from the page head in force: the entry's own head, or `history.state.page` under a link head.
- **`t` without `th` for an in-panel thread** (`tlInPanel`): the router adds `th=open` to that head by `replaceState` (stamped), so the panel opens on it.
- **Under a `talk/` head** the card is the link's thread; a Threads change that changes `t` there (opening another thread, or dropping it: the back arrow, the card's close, Escape) replaces the `talk/` entry with the page head under it plus the new `th` and `t` (stamped `replaceState`), so the one card shows one thread; a change that keeps `t` (picking Open or Resolved, opening the panel) is `withPage` as for any link head.
- **A `talk/<id>` link** to an in-panel thread opens the panel on that thread over the page it was clicked on (cold: Overview): kit-87's in-place rewrite of the `talk/` entry's recorded page gets `th=open&t=<id>`. A `talk/` link to a thread with a subject keeps kit-87's behaviour (its card over its subject's page), with `t` added to that page head.
- **Remembered** (`devteam.threads.open`, one `localStorage` key, `{ th, t? }` or absent; shell.md §8 "the Threads panel's open state"): written on every route from the page head in force (under a link head, `history.state.page`), its `th` and, with it, `t`; absent when the panel is closed. Opening a peek or the viewer doesn't clear it, since the page head under it still has the keys. Only an in-panel `t` is stored with `th`: a floating thread's subject is on one page, so carrying its `t` to the next page would open it in the wrong place; for a floating `t`, only `th` is stored. Applied only to a fresh entry whose page head in force (under a link head, the page head under it) carries neither `th` nor `t`; freshness is read before kit-87 stamps the entry. **Fresh** (shell.md §8) is an entry with no `history.state` that the Threads code didn't make: `thGo` records the hash it is about to assign (`TH_MADE = head`) before assigning it, and the router, on an entry with no state whose hash equals `TH_MADE`, clears the mark and treats it as a Threads change (not fresh); every other stateless entry (a cold load, a nav click, a link) is fresh. **Order** on each route: (1) on a fresh entry, apply the store: on a page head, write the stored keys into the hash by stamped `replaceState`; on a link head (a cold-loaded peek link), into `history.state.page` with `withPage`, the hash left as the link; (2) draw; (3) write the store from the head now in force. So × closing the panel (a Threads change, not fresh) writes "closed" to the store and the panel stays closed, and a nav click afterwards (fresh) finds nothing stored; a nav click with the panel open (fresh, its head without keys) carries it to the next page. So the panel stays open, on the same thread, as the owner moves between pages, and a fresh visit opens it as they left it; an entry reached by Back or Forward shows exactly its own keys. A stored `t` for a thread `/api/threads` doesn't have is dropped (the list shows). A throwing store does nothing.
- **Decisions that need the threads.** Whether a `t` is known, in-panel or floating (`tlInPanel`), and so whether it is stored, gets `th=open` added, or is dropped, needs `/api/threads`. Until its first read has landed (`TL.have > 0`), the router keeps `th` and `t` exactly as the head has them (the panel shows the list's loading line; no card opens; nothing is dropped and nothing is stored), and when that read lands the Threads part of the route runs again (these decisions, the panel's draw, the store), never the whole route (no peek reopened, no subject scrolled twice), so a fresh tab opened on a remembered `t` keeps it.
- **Bad values.** The block's `parseHead` keeps any value of a state key, and the block doesn't change, so the page's router checks them after `parseHead`: `th` must be `open` or `resolved` (`inbox` never reaches here; kit-87's redirect takes it first) and `t` a thread id (`^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]{0,79}$`). A bad value is dropped from the hash by `replaceState` (stamped), and the control shows its default (closed, no thread).

## 4. Edge cases

- **No threads at all:** no thread count on the button, Threads' count empty, the list's empty line; the panel still opens.
- **Pending sends:** a turn the owner just sent is on the thread before the server has it (today's overlay); it makes the thread "waiting on the supervisor" at once, so the counts move with the press.
- **A thread resolved while open** (by the owner or elsewhere): it stays shown, with Reopen, until the owner goes back. A resolved thread named by `t` still opens, after a reload too; only an id `/api/threads` doesn't have is dropped. The remembered `t` follows the same rule.
- **A `t` for a thread not yet read** (a cold load before `/api/threads` answers): the panel shows the list's loading state and opens the thread once the read lands; an id the read doesn't have is dropped from the hash by `replaceState`.
- **The 10-second redraw and the threads poll** while typing in the in-panel composer: the card sits in `#th-card-slot`, which no draw rewrites, and moves only when its place changes, so the focus, caret and words stay (the draft store keeps them across a reload).
- **Shift+click inside the panel**: the panel is the layer's own (§2.1), and `tlHitAt` gives no subject over it; the panel is not a thread target.
- **Pin mode** with the panel open: pointing at the page works as today; the panel and the button are not targets. At 900px and below (the panel covers the page) Pin closes the panel first (`thGo` without `th`), then starts pin mode.
- **A + draft becoming its thread**: words typed in the composer after Send (held under the draft's `pending-N` key) carry over to the real id's draft, and the composer keeps them and the focus.
- **Focus on a redraw that removes it**: if a pane draw removes the focused element for a change not covered by §2.1's placement (a floating row clicked, Back), the focus goes to the row of the thread now current if there is one, else the selected tab; never to `<body>`.
- **Narrow screens:** below 900px the panel is the full width; closing it is the ×; the floating card is a bottom sheet under 640px; the button is at the window's top right.
- **Reduced motion:** `--dur` is 0, so the panel and the in-panel view change with no motion; the in-panel view swap uses no animation of its own (the mock's sideways slide is left out: the block names only the panel and peek slides).
- **A `#talk/` link while the panel is open** to a subject thread: the panel stays open (its `th` is kept on the page head), the card opens beside its subject.
- **kit-145** (artifact-annotations' "to check" badge on today's dock row): not landed when this was written. Whichever of the two lands second carries the badge into the new row (ticket note); if kit-145 has landed when this builds, the row gets it here.
