| F1-2 | Important | ticket notes: "kills process trees on shared machines"; design §Cleanup step 1 | spec.md §Watchdog and cleanup, step 1; §Edge cases, last bullet | "SIGKILL them all" kills every pid ever seen. That includes a pid that exited between a snapshot and its SIGSTOP and could have been reused by an unrelated process. "Adding new descendants of any known pid" also adopts the children of such a reused pid. The child's own pid can be reused the same way when the watchdog fires as the child exits and is reaped. The risk is small but real on a shared box, and the spec's own rule ("kill only pids found under the child") isn't met by the algorithm as written. Make the SIGKILL set the pids that the final snapshot still shows as stopped (`ps -o pid=,ppid=,stat=`, state `T`) and that were stopped by this walk. Walk only from pids confirmed stopped. | F1-3 | Important | design §Cleanup: "On SIGINT … so a Ctrl-C leaves nothing behind"; done-when 6 | spec.md §Watchdog and cleanup (interrupt), §Acceptance checks 6 | A terminal Ctrl-C sends SIGINT to the whole foreground process group, not only to `run.mjs`. Test it by sending SIGINT to the runner's process group as a real terminal would, not to its pid alone. | |

| F1-5 | Important | design §Cleanup step 2 (sockets "under the scratch folder"); constitution's shared-box safety (the kill-server that takes down every live fleet, `lib/kit.mjs` lines 36-40) | spec.md §Watchdog and cleanup, step 2 | "Every socket under the scratch folder (a recursive walk)" doesn't say the walk must not follow symlinks.
